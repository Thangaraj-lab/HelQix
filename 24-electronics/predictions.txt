E1 — DC analysis (written before any E1 code)
---------------------------------------------
Prediction:
- The hand-calculated divider, bridge, Thevenin/Norton, superposition and
  10-node ladder cases will agree to better than 1e-12 relative with sparse
  MNA in float64, i.e. inside the 1e-9 target.
- The dominant risk is sign convention on current sources and VCVS control
  nodes, not arithmetic. Hand formulas in tests will pin the convention.
- Floating-node detection and voltage-source-loop detection will need
  topology checks before factorization; relying on a singular-matrix error
  alone would give vague messages.
- Expected runtime of the E1 suite: under 2 s.

E2 — Transient / AC / nonlinear (written before any E2 code)
------------------------------------------------------------
Prediction:
- Trapezoidal RC charge at h = T/200 will be within 0.02% of the analytic
  curve; backward Euler will be within 0.5% only at the same step, so the
  0.5% target is reachable with both methods at modest step counts.
- RLC resonance with Q around 5 will be within 0.2% of 1/(2*pi*sqrt(LC))
  when the sweep has 200 points/decade plus bisection refinement.
- Newton on a diode with series resistance will converge in under 15
  iterations with damping; without damping it will overflow exp() on a
  direct 5 V step. Expect to need a voltage limiter.
- MOSFET level-1 saturation current will match the formula to float64
  round-off (it is the same formula) inside 1e-12.
- Op-amp gain target 0.1% is easy for the finite-gain model; the ideal
  model needs a nullor constraint, not a 1e9 VCVS, or the matrix becomes
  badly conditioned.
- AC cutoff search by bisection will hit 1% easily; a 20-point/decade grid
  alone may miss by 1–2% near the knee.
- Risk: adaptive stepping can reject forever near a switching event.
  Mitigation: minimum step and a hard max_steps report.
- Expected suite runtime: under 10 s.

E3 — Harness (written before any E3 code)
-----------------------------------------
Prediction:
- R = rho*L/A will match the hand formula to 1e-15 relative; the 1e-6
  target is limited only by unit conversion (mm, mm^2, AWG).
- AWG conversion will be the main error source; the exact formula
  d_mm = 0.127 * 92^((36-n)/39) will be written in the test.
- Verdict logic will be the main correctness risk at boundaries; tests will
  probe just below, exactly at, and just above each limit.
- Ampacity: I will use IEC 60364-5-52 Table B.52.2 rows for PVC, 2 loaded
  conductors, 30 C ambient. Sizes not in the cited table will be UNKNOWN and
  get no ampacity verdict, per the no-invented-data rule.
- Temperature rise with h = 10 W/(m^2 K) is an ASSUMPTION; the number will
  be labelled as such and will not be used to claim safety.
- Expected suite runtime: under 2 s.

E4 — Sensors (written before any E4 code)
-----------------------------------------
Prediction:
- White noise scaled by density*sqrt(fs/2) through the discrete one-pole
  filter will give a measured sigma within 1% of the analytic
  density*sqrt(ENBW) prediction at 100000 samples (statistical error about
  0.22%), comfortably inside 3%.
- Quantization step will be exact to float64 representation; tests will
  compare multiples within 1e-12 absolute.
- The digital one-pole -3 dB point will land within 0.5% of the stated
  bandwidth for fc/fs <= 0.1; at fc/fs = 0.4 the bilinear-free one-pole
  will drift several percent, so tests will use the documented valid range.
- Seeded output will be byte-identical because no wall-clock or hash-order
  randomness is used.
- Reality gap will be dominated by noise and delay, not quantization for a
  12-bit sensor.
- Expected suite runtime: under 3 s (100k-sample tests marked nightly may be
  slower).

E5 — Interface (written before any E5 code)
-------------------------------------------
Prediction:
- Exit codes 0/2/3 can be enforced without catching programmer errors by
  validating input into typed exceptions at the boundary.
- Timeout enforcement inside Python loops is reliable for our step counts;
  a hard kill is not portable without processes, so timeout_s will be
  checked between steps and reported as solver failure (exit 3).
- Downsampling to 2000 points with min/max envelope per bucket preserves
  spikes better than plain decimation.
- Fresh work folder: writing only results.json, series CSV and a manifest
  will be verifiable by listing DIR before/after.
- Expected CLI runtime per small case: under 1 s.

E5 — outcome (kept honestly, written after the phase)
------------------------------------------------------
- Exit codes held: every probe produced 0, 2 or 3 with no traceback. Typos
  in case keys are now refused (exit 2) instead of ignored.
- Timeout: first attempt used a watchdog thread that hard-killed the run.
  WRONG in practice: the daemon thread kept executing numpy at interpreter
  shutdown and the process died with exit 120 and a SystemError. Replaced
  with cooperative deadline checks inside the transient and AC loops; a
  case that finishes after the deadline is reported as solver_error
  (exit 3) with "timeout" in the message, as predicted.
- Downsampling: predicted a min/max envelope. Actual implementation is a
  rounded linspace index (2000 evenly spaced samples, endpoints kept,
  duplicates removed). Simple and exact on the endpoints, and the tests
  pin first/last point and strict monotonicity; spikes between samples can
  be missed, so the envelope idea remains the better long-term option.
  Prediction kept as a deviation.
- Fresh folder: works for an empty or foreign folder; re-running over our
  own outputs is allowed by deleting only the three known output files.
- CLI runtime: MEASURED in-process small case about 0.02 s; one real
  `python -m` subprocess start costs about 1 s on this laptop, so the
  subprocess tests were cut to one launch per package.
- Unknown key handling was not predicted; the adversarial review found that
  `limts` was silently ignored, so both CLIs now reject unknown case keys.

E7 — Virtual I/O (written before any E7 code)
---------------------------------------------
Prediction:
- 10000 in-memory framed messages round-trip exactly in under 2 s.
- Ordering is guaranteed by FIFO queues per endpoint as long as fault
  injection does not reorder; delay injection may reorder, so ordering
  tests will use no-delay mode.
- Retry after disconnect needs an application-level ack with a message id;
  a bare queue cannot know a message was lost.
- Timeout behaviour will use real short timeouts (10–50 ms) and may be
  flaky on a busy laptop; tests will allow a generous upper bound.
- A real MQTT broker is not installable under the allowed dependency list,
  so that cross-check will be UNKNOWN.

E7 — outcome (written after the phase)
--------------------------------------
- Round trips: 10000 serial frames in 0.049 s and 10000 bus messages in
  0.080 s, all payloads identical and in order. The 2 s prediction was
  beaten by a wide margin. The 10000-message tests stay in the fast suite.
- Ordering: the prediction that delay injection may reorder was wrong. A
  per-port delay is one constant added to every frame, so FIFO order is
  preserved; the seeded property tests exercise random delay up to 2 ms and
  assert the delivered frames are an in-order subsequence.
- Retry after disconnect: as predicted, the bare queue could not know a
  message was lost, so QoS 1 keeps a pending entry with a message id until
  `ack()`; `retry_unacked()` resends under a new id. Unlike MQTT persistent
  sessions the broker does not queue for a disconnected client; this is
  documented rather than hidden.
- Timeouts: 20–50 ms with generous upper bounds; no flake across the verify
  runs, so the predicted flakiness did not appear on this laptop.
- Real broker: the prediction was wrong. amqtt 0.12.2 and paho-mqtt
  installed cleanly in a throwaway venv (network available, no admin). A
  probe against a real broker compared 49 filter/topic pairs with
  `topic_matches` and found zero disagreements. It is not part of the
  offline suite because it needs network to install; wire-protocol
  compatibility stays UNKNOWN as deliberate scope.
- Mutation harness found my first "corrupted frame delivered" mutation was
  behaviourally equivalent (the frame was still skipped); rewrote it to
  actually deliver the frame, after which all 57 mutations are caught. Kept
  here because the harness catching a bad mutation is itself a result.
