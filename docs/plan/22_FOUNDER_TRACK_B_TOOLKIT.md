# 22. Founder Track B Toolkit (things Claude Code cannot do for you)

Track B runs in parallel with the build and decides whether the product is worth building at all. Claude Code can write code; it cannot find customers.

## B1. Engineer interviews (target: 5 before Phase 1 ends)

Who: engineers who run simulations and have to justify results (battery pack, EV, drone, industrial motor, thermal, test/validation teams). Not students.
Goal: test hypotheses H1 to H6 in 08. Learn, do not pitch.

Question bank (ask about the past, not hypotheticals):
1. Tell me about the last time a simulation result was questioned. What happened?
2. How do you decide a model is good enough? Who signs off? What evidence is kept?
3. When a test fails, how long to find the cause? What tools do you use?
4. How do you reproduce a result someone else got six months ago?
5. What do you pay for today in this area? Roughly how much per year?
6. Who would approve buying a tool like this? Who blocks it?
7. What would make you not trust a tool like this?
Record: role, company type, exact words, current tools, budget owner. Do not lead the witness.

Success signal: at least 2 of 5 describe the pain unprompted and name a current workaround with a cost. Weak signal: polite interest.

## B2. Pilot commitment test

Ask for something costly to say yes to: a real dataset, 2 hours of an engineer's time per week for 4 weeks, or a signed letter of intent. Kill trigger from 06: 0 of 3 pilots commit means re-evaluate the wedge.

## B3. Prior-art and patent check (do before claiming novelty)

Search: Ansys Minerva, Aras and other SPDM tools, FMI/FMU state handling, record-and-replay in simulation, digital thread products, reproducibility platforms (for example workflow provenance systems). Record what exists, what overlaps, what HELQIX adds. Patent search should be done with a patent attorney if you intend to file; I did not search patent databases (UNKNOWN). Do not market "nobody does this".

## B4. Rig plan (if battery or small-motor wedge)

Use the draft in 08. Founder tasks: choose the exact parts, verify datasheet limits, write the safety procedure, define the test protocol (D4), collect first dataset. Lithium safety is a hard requirement. Get an experienced person to review the rig before powering it.

## B5. Naming and legal basics

D6: HELIX vs HELQIX (check trademark conflicts in your country and target markets; I did not run a trademark search). Entity and IP ownership between team members: write down who owns what (college rules may claim IP; check your institution's policy).

## B6. Funding and money (D11)

Options to evaluate with facts at the time: college incubator, government startup schemes, cloud credits, small angel, customer-funded pilot. I did not verify any current scheme; research when needed. Early target: customer-funded pilot is the strongest validation.

## B7. Cadence

Weekly: 1 interview or outreach batch, 1 build gate review, 1 status board update. Monthly: re-run the kill-trigger review.
